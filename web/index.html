      The numbers behind it

      This replays a real week of US power data (Oct 19–25, 2026, from the U.S. Energy Information Administration) that the model never saw while learning. Every prediction here is the real model's; the scheduling runs live on your phone. "Rush hour" = demand within 5% of that day's high. The data centers, how full they are and the utility's call are simulated; the jobs are simulated too, but in the real mix of Alibaba's 2026 trace of 155,410 GPUs; the swing numbers scale up one measured chip.

      * Dollars: Rocky Mountain Power's Wattsmart Business program pays $100 per kW a year for load it can cut on call, up to 65 hours a year. This is one week's share for the kW cut, plus Utah's real wholesale price for each kWh not used (CAISO real-time market, PacifiCorp East). The call itself (Tue, noon–2 PM) is simulated: Rocky Mountain Power lists its real calls in an annual report to the Utah PSC, and none fell in this week. Its real Wattsmart Business calls run 15–60 minutes (24 in 2023–25); the replay stretches this one to 2 hours so you can see it. kWh are computed from the measured chip power. Homes use the EIA's 2024 Utah average of 774 kWh a month (about 1.06 kW). The Utah data center (1,000 chips) and its jobs are simulated (the job mix follows Alibaba's 2026 GPU trace); the grid data, the prices and every prediction are real.
